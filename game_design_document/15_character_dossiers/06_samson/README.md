# Samson

## Core Identity
- Samson is Aen's brother-in-arms: direct, physical, loyal, and emotionally honest under pressure.
- He should feel like someone who meets fear by stepping toward it, not by pretending it is gone.
- His support style is active presence, blunt truth, and shared risk.

## Narrative Function
- Samson turns brotherhood into gameplay instead of keeping it as background sentiment.
- He proves that courage after the wound is not solo bravado but practiced trust and mutual presence.
- He is one of the clearest bridges between early warmth and later recovery, because his support is legible in both sparring and rescue.

## What They Represent
- Samson represents brotherhood, pressure honesty, embodied courage, and the refusal to leave someone alone inside danger.
- He represents the kind of support that challenges Aen without flattering him.
- He also represents strength that can admit fear and still act.

## How They Express That In Play
- In Lantern Heights, Samson should introduce or reinforce combat through a friendly spar that feels warm rather than competitive. The lesson is that pressure can be honest and safe at the same time.
- Later rematches or training encounters should sharpen timing, spacing, and commitment under pressure. Samson's dialogue and encounter design should tell Aen the truth when he is hiding behind posture or anger.
- Low-health courage beats should be tied to Samson's support logic. When the player survives on the edge, Samson-related content should frame courage as staying present rather than as reckless damage racing.
- Rescue and support beats should be concrete. Samson should help extract Aen, hold a line, or open a path at moments where brotherhood is best expressed through action, not speeches.

## Gameplay Impact
- Samson's dossier should matter to combat readability. Sparring and pressure-honesty encounters should leave the player better at reading spacing, recovery, and commitment.
- His support should make low-health states feel interpretable rather than purely punitive, reinforcing courage without glamorizing near-death play.
- Rescue beats keep brotherhood systemic: the player learns that help can arrive inside gameplay and that accepting it is part of Aen's arc.
- He also keeps courage distinct from Yang. Yang is lost spirit-fire; Samson is lived, human support under stress.

## Player Learning
- The player learns that pressure can clarify rather than humiliate.
- Samson teaches that courage is not clean confidence; it is staying engaged when panic would be easier.
- His presence teaches that being helped in combat or escape does not invalidate Aen's strength.

## Act-by-Act Presence
- Lantern Heights: friendly sparring, warmth, and visible mutual trust.
- Summit Shrine and Hollow Depths: absence sharpens the cost of isolation.
- Ember Monastery and support arc: Samson returns through training, rescue, and honest confrontation.
- Skyroad and final climb: his brotherhood should echo in optional encouragement, gauntlets, or pre-final support beats.
- Beacon resolution: Samson's role confirms that Aen was rebuilt with others, not in spite of them.

## System Dependencies
- Duel or spar scripting, combat tutorial structure, and rematch states.
- Low-health dialogue or state-response hooks.
- Rescue encounter staging and side-quest progression flags.
- Combat-expression framing that distinguishes courage from aggression.

## Failure Mode
- Samson fails if he becomes a generic rival or loud tough friend.
- He also fails if sparring has no lasting gameplay value or if rescue beats are replaced by pure dialogue reassurance.
- If his courage reads like reckless violence instead of shared pressure and presence, the dossier drifts.
