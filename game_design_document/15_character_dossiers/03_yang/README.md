# Yang

## Core Identity
- Yang is Aen's bright, warm, forward-driving spirit-orb: the visible embodiment of courage, motion, and fire.
- He should feel eager, protective, and momentum-oriented without becoming reckless comic relief.
- After the Hollowing, Yang persists only as absence, star motif, and remembered ignition rather than as a restored attack tool.

## Narrative Function
- Yang establishes what brave forward motion feels like before the wound.
- His removal strips drive and confidence from Aen at the same time the story strips visible balance through Yin.
- His continued star-state helps the ending mean future-facing hope rather than recovered possession.

## What They Represent
- Yang represents courage, action, risk acceptance, initiative, and the will to move even when certainty is incomplete.
- He is the fire that turns potential into step, leap, strike, and decision.
- Later, he represents the painful distance between remembered vitality and the quieter courage Aen must relearn for himself.

## How They Express That In Play
- In Lantern Heights, Yang should be visible in Aen's bold rhythm: quick follow-through on jumps, combat readiness, chase energy, and the feeling that movement naturally wants to continue.
- Early tutorial and first-threat encounters should let Yang's presence color risk-taking. Optional rooftops, short combat flurries, and first forward pushes should feel warmer and more immediate because he is there. Pre-loss challenge reads can use Yang cues to mark fast routes, momentum-preserving jumps, and attack windows where immediate commitment is rewarded.
- Yang should also have encounter-facing timing logic. In early combat and traversal drills, his cueing can emphasize burst timing by brightening during ideal chase windows, aggressive follow-ups, or courage-coded leap opportunities that close distance instead of resetting to safety.
- After the scripted loss, Yang should not come back as a summoned damage source. He should survive as distant gold star imagery, high-route hope signals, and remembered fire in moments that ask the player to keep climbing without guarantee. Post-loss Yang-star guidance can mark high-risk shortcuts, optional gauntlet entries, or routes that trade safety for speed and confidence.
- Late-game challenge paths and sky-facing beats should use Yang-coded logic to change encounter consequences: taking his marked route can front-load enemy pressure, compress recovery windows, or unlock faster traversal rewards, while refusing it leaves slower but safer progress. The player should feel his absence most when choosing to keep moving anyway.

## Gameplay Impact
- Yang's early presence should teach the player what confident forward momentum feels like before the wound, including which routes, attack strings, and traversal sequences reward decisive commitment.
- His cueing should directly affect risk interpretation. When Yang logic is present, the player can read burst windows, forward-pressure routes, and courage-tested shortcuts; when he is absent, advancing aggressively becomes less legible and more emotionally costly.
- His absence should alter how risk reads. Post-loss bravery should feel learned and costly, not naturally supplied, and later Yang-star markers should make clear that high-risk opportunities still exist even though the instinctive fire behind them is gone.
- His star motif should shape ending interpretation: accepting play reads like matured courage, while domination reads like courage curdled into force.
- He also helps separate "upward drive" from generic traversal by keeping it emotionally coded.

## Player Learning
- The player learns that courage can be visible, warm, and shared early in the game.
- Losing Yang teaches that drive is not the same thing as violence or speed alone.
- His continued star presence teaches that hope can remain distant and still be real.

## Act-by-Act Presence
- Lantern Heights: Yang is visible, energizing, and bound to Aen's first combat and traversal confidence.
- Summit Shrine: Yang is isolated with Aen, then taken during the Hollowing as the active forward spirit is severed.
- Hollow Depths: Yang is absent, and forward motion must be rebuilt without his immediate heat.
- Ember Monastery and Skyroad: Yang returns only as starward aspiration and risk-affirming visual language.
- Mirror Summit and Beacon Cliff: Yang remains a star, confirming that future drive survives without being reclaimed as an owned object.

## System Dependencies
- Pre-loss companion VFX tied to traversal and combat readability.
- Challenge-route, skyline, and star-motif environmental signaling.
- Post-loss risk framing in level language and ending interpretation layers.
- Summit Shrine scripting that makes his removal permanent within the campaign timeline.

## Failure Mode
- Yang fails if he is reduced to generic bravery or aggression without a clear movement language.
- He also fails if his absence does not meaningfully change how risk and momentum feel after Summit Shrine.
- If his star motif reads as a promise of getting him back instead of learning to continue without him, the dossier misses the narrative guardrail.
