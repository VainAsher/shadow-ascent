# Aen

## Core Identity
- Aen is a gifted young ninja from Lantern Heights whose twin spirit-orbs, Yin and Yang, make him visibly exceptional from the start.
- His core wound is not that he begins broken. He begins already whole, but is made to believe that wholeness only matters if someone outside him confirms it.
- The dossier should always treat "The Hollowed Ninja" as a state imposed on Aen, not his truest nature.

## Narrative Function
- Aen is the player vessel through the full campaign arc: warmth, praise, isolation, collapse, recovery, ascent, release, and future-facing wholeness.
- He is the character through whom the game proves its thesis that loss is not undone, but can be lived with without surrendering selfhood.
- His state should drive the emotional reading of hubs, levels, movement recovery, and ending tone.

## What They Represent
- Aen represents wholeness misread as specialness, then relearned as relationship, discipline, and chosen support.
- He represents grief that first hardens into emptiness, then becomes survivable through practical help rather than prophecy.
- He also represents the difference between external validation and internal integration.

## How They Express That In Play
- In Lantern Heights, Aen should feel warm, agile, and socially held. Rooftop routes, lantern-line jumps, friendly sparring, and the first combat encounters all happen in a context where Yin and Yang are visible and his motion still belongs to home.
- The Veil Maiden's manipulation should arrive through mission structure, not just dialogue. Her requests pull Aen away from communal loops, prioritize solo errands, and steadily make isolation feel like destiny rather than a red flag.
- At Summit Shrine, the scripted Hollowing must take Yin and Yang from him in a way that reframes the whole moveset. The loss is not a normal boss loss; it is the violent removal of the emotional context that made early movement and combat feel safe.
- In the Hollow Depths and Ember Monastery stretch, regained abilities should come back through support-bearing encounters: brotherhood, truthful guidance, repair, warmth, drills, and structure. Dash, double jump, glide, grapple, wall-cling mastery, and air-dodge should each read as recovery carried by someone or something outside pure self-assertion.
- In the late game, Aen's combat expression must split cleanly between armed pressure and unarmed restraint. Mirror Summit and the Hollow Reflection fight should let the player feel whether Aen is forcing control through hurt or releasing control through mastery.

## Gameplay Impact
- Aen's movement unlocks are the recovery arc. Each regained verb changes traversal, but it also changes the emotional meaning of continuing.
- Combat expression changes ending feel. A player who resolves the late game through hard lethal pressure should reach the Beacon with a harsher tone than a player who proves mastery through restraint.
- Aen's current state should directly tune hub warmth, light-state presentation, NPC density, and progression tone. Lantern Heights, the Hollow Depths, Ember Monastery, Skyroad, and Beacon Cliff should all feel like changes in Aen made spatial.
- His dossier is the anchor for reading every other Tier 1 character, because their mechanics matter insofar as they alter what Aen can do, how he reads danger, and how the world answers him.

## Player Learning
- Early game teaches the player that Aen is capable before he is wounded.
- The Hollowing teaches that the campaign is not about becoming "more chosen," but surviving forced emptiness.
- Midgame teaches that progress returns through support, routine, witness, and earned practice.
- Endgame teaches that being whole again does not mean reclaiming Yin and Yang as possessions.

## Act-by-Act Presence
- Lantern Heights: Aen is warm, admired, physically fluid, and still embedded in communal movement and beginner combat.
- Summit Shrine: Aen is singled out, separated, praised into vulnerability, then hollowed through the scripted loss of Yin and Yang.
- Hollow Depths: Aen survives with diminished certainty and slowly reclaims agency one ability at a time.
- Ember Monastery: Aen rebuilds through mentors and peers, with support encounters broadening motion into mastery.
- Winding Skyroad: Aen integrates the full kit under the visible absence of Yin and Yang as stars rather than companions.
- Mirror Summit and Beacon Cliff: Aen proves whether mastery becomes domination or release, then carries light forward without reversal.

## System Dependencies
- Ability unlock and progression flag sequencing.
- Hub-state changes, light-state presentation, and NPC availability.
- Scripted Summit Shrine Hollowing and post-loss presentation rules.
- Combat expression tracking for late-game tone and ending texture.
- Dialogue, encounter, and tutorial content that frame support as a gameplay source rather than background flavor.

## Failure Mode
- Aen fails as a dossier if he reads like a standard "broken hero who gets stronger" arc.
- He also fails if his starting wholeness, Veil Maiden-driven isolation, or support-driven recovery are left implicit instead of mechanically visible.
- The late game collapses if armed pressure and unarmed restraint feel emotionally identical.
