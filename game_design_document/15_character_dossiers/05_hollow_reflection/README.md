# Hollow Reflection

## Core Identity
- Hollow Reflection is Aen's wound wearing Aen's shape.
- It is not an unrelated monster and not a substitute villain for the Siren; it is the form grief, rage, emptiness, and obsession take when survival hardens into identity.
- Its silhouette, movement, and combat language should all read as "Aen if pain became his only truth."

## Narrative Function
- Hollow Reflection is the true final boss and the campaign's decisive emotional exam.
- It tests whether the player's recovered mastery serves domination or release.
- It converts every support-earned tool in the game into a question: what happens when community-taught strength is turned inward and hostile?

## What They Represent
- Hollow Reflection represents unresolved rage, survival as armor, and the belief that wholeness can only come from taking back what was lost.
- It represents the temptation to answer pain with control, punishment, and self-erasure.
- It is the story's clearest warning that healing and hardening can share the same exterior moves.

## How They Express That In Play
- The fight should mirror Aen's recovered toolkit directly: dashes, second reaches, aerial corrections, wall pressure, plunges, and upward re-engagements should all appear in hostile form.
- Support-earned lessons should return as threats. Community-made mobility, endurance, and combat options should feel twisted into proof that pain can imitate growth.
- The arena and phase structure should force the player to recognize familiar verbs under hostile intent. This is how the boss proves that the deepest enemy is not lack of skill, but what the recovered self chooses to become.
- The final resolution needs a meaningful split between domination and release. A player leaning into armed pressure should be able to win, but the fight and aftermath should feel harsher than a mastery-through-restraint resolution.

## Gameplay Impact
- Hollow Reflection determines final encounter feel because it mirrors the full integrated kit instead of introducing a disconnected spectacle rule set.
- It turns community-earned skills hostile, making earlier support characters matter again at the point of climax.
- It is the strongest system bridge between combat expression and ending texture: domination should feel like victory without full release, while restraint should feel like the most complete thematic resolution.
- The boss also validates the entire recovery arc by proving that regained power alone is morally insufficient.

## Player Learning
- The player learns that mastery can be used to hold pain tighter or to release it.
- The fight teaches that the game's final challenge is interpretive, not merely mechanical.
- The ending teaches that defeating the hollow self is not the same as annihilating grief.

## Act-by-Act Presence
- Early game: present only as a future possibility implied by the wound, not a visible enemy.
- Hollow Depths and Ember Monastery: foreshadowed through echoes, harsher self-readings, and the risk of building identity entirely around hurt.
- Winding Skyroad: increasingly legible as the version of Aen that could emerge from integrated skill without integrated meaning.
- Mirror Summit: fully embodied as the true final confrontation.
- Beacon Cliff: absent as an enemy but present in the tonal difference between domination and release outcomes.

## System Dependencies
- Full late-game moveset support and boss AI that can mirror player verbs.
- Combat expression tracking for lethal pressure versus restraint.
- Encounter scripting and ending-state presentation at Mirror Summit and Beacon Cliff.
- Foreshadowing content that clarifies the Reflection is internal, not a replacement lore villain.

## Failure Mode
- Hollow Reflection fails if it behaves like a standard dark-double boss with no connection to Aen's actual toolkit.
- It also fails if domination and release read the same in mechanics or aftermath.
- If support characters do not echo through the fight's verbs, the final boss loses its community-earned stakes.
