# Yin

## Core Identity
- Yin is Aen's still, cool, intuitive spirit-orb: the visible embodiment of softness, balance, and restraint.
- She should feel present, observant, and grounding rather than chatty or utility-like.
- After the Hollowing, Yin survives as sacred absence, witness-state, star motif, and interpretive lens rather than an active companion.

## Narrative Function
- Yin establishes what emotional balance looks like before the wound.
- Her removal is one half of the campaign's central severing, proving that Aen loses more than power at Summit Shrine.
- Her continued absence helps the ending mean acceptance instead of restoration, and her later traces should frame calm, safety, and restraint rather than route-finding.

## What They Represent
- Yin represents stillness, caution, receptivity, tenderness, and the ability to remain with pain without immediately attacking it.
- She is the part of Aen that can read safety, listen, witness what is true without forcing it, and hold memory without distortion.
- Later, she represents the ache of what cannot be re-possessed.

## How They Express That In Play
- In Lantern Heights, Yin should be visibly readable around Aen during pauses, ledge checks, rest moments, and calm traversal. Her motion language should make balance legible before the player knows to miss it.
- Early safe spaces, quiet tutorial beats, and memory-friendly hub interactions should feel subtly protected by Yin's presence through UI, VFX, or pacing cues rather than exposition alone. Pre-loss save rooms, shrine benches, or home hearth spaces can use a Yin cue to signal no ambushes, no hostile spawn pressure, and full emotional decompression before the wound.
- Yin should carry witness-state mechanics rather than cartography. In memory rooms and shrine puzzles, her cueing can distinguish emotionally true echoes from manipulative false images by holding a steady glow near the correct witness object, safe platform, or restraint-valid interaction target while decoys flicker or remain cold.
- After the scripted loss, Yin should not return as a mechanic pet. Instead, she should persist as cool star motifs, memory-room echoes, and calm sky markers that help the player interpret safety, honesty, and emotional truth. Post-loss Yin-star alignment should mark recovery-space entrances, nonviolent puzzle solutions, and witness-based interaction points, not broad route correction.
- Optional memory encounters and later reflective spaces should use Yin-coded logic to change encounter consequences: rooms flagged by her cues can suppress surprise attacks, reveal hidden witness lore, unlock restraint-based completions, or open quiet exits that are unavailable when the player resolves the room through pure aggression.

## Gameplay Impact
- Yin's early presence should make safety readable. The player learns what calm, protected, truthful space feels like before the game removes it, and can reliably associate Yin-coded rooms with shelter, checkpoint confidence, and honest witness framing.
- Her cueing should directly affect encounter readability. When Yin logic is present, players can identify safe recovery pockets, true memory objects, lower-conflict exits, and restraint-valid resolutions faster; when she is absent, rooms become more ambiguous and emotionally colder to parse.
- Her absence should change how the player reads memory and shelter. Later rooms that echo Yin should feel like witness and emotional interpretation, not navigation restoration, and can materially reward patient observation with preserved healing, safer decompression, or restraint-specific outcomes.
- Ending interpretation should sharpen through Yin's continued star-state: a restrained resolution feels aligned with her meaning, while harsher play styles feel more distant from her logic.
- She also helps distinguish emotional categories in the world, keeping "quiet" from collapsing into "empty."

## Player Learning
- The player learns stillness as an active quality, not a passive lack of action.
- The loss of Yin teaches that calm and safety were part of Aen's life before the wound, not optional sentiment layered on top.
- Her star motif teaches the player to read absence as sacred continuation rather than as a missing collectible.

## Act-by-Act Presence
- Lantern Heights: Yin is visible, immediate, and entwined with warmth, safety, and beginner confidence.
- Summit Shrine: Yin becomes vulnerable inside the Veil Maiden's isolating frame and is taken during the Hollowing.
- Hollow Depths: Yin is absent as active presence, with only memory contrast showing what is gone.
- Ember Monastery and Skyroad: Yin returns as cool star logic, memory echoes, and restraint-coded spaces.
- Mirror Summit and Beacon Cliff: Yin remains a star, clarifying that acceptance does not equal reunion.

## System Dependencies
- Pre-loss companion presentation and idle/movement VFX.
- Safe-room, memory-room, witness-state, and star-motif environmental signaling.
- Ending tone, dialogue, and visual interpretation layers.
- Summit Shrine scripting that clearly marks her removal as irreversible within the campaign.

## Failure Mode
- Yin fails if she is reduced to a vague moonlike mood instead of a readable safety and memory language.
- She also fails if her post-loss presence behaves like a soft promise of restoration.
- If players cannot tell how Yin-shaped spaces differ from generic calm spaces, the dossier is under-specified.
