# Character Movement

## Current Repo-Backed Truth
Aen already supports the movement verbs most central to the game identity: running, precision walking, jumping, double-jumping, wall-jumping, wall-sliding, dashing, fast-falling, attacking, and interacting.

## Broader Movement Arc
The story and quest flags imply a larger restoration path that expands movement capability over the campaign, including glide, grapple, wall cling mastery, and air dodge.

## Design Reading
Movement is not just locomotion. It is a major emotional and progression language in the project, with restoration of ability tied to recovery, ascent, and release.

## Combat-Movement Thesis
Movement should also be treated as Aen's primary defense layer. The game is strongest when enemies read as movement problems before they read as health bars. That means:
- dash is repositioning under pressure, not only speed
- grapple creates aerial routes through combat space
- wall cling and wall jump create vertical combat layers
- glide preserves flow when space collapses
- double jump creates escape and re-entry vectors

This gives the game a traversal-combat identity rather than a stand-still combo identity.

## Feel Direction
The intended movement blend is:
- acrobatic readability and chaining first
- emotional weight second
- precision gating third

In practical terms, movement should feel closer to expressive traversal than to rigid combat-footing. Aen should feel agile and intentional even when armed, but armed movement should still feel more committed and dangerous than unarmed movement.

## Thematic Mapping
- **Dash** should feel like practical support turning paralysis into motion.
- **Double Jump** should feel like the second reach after collapse.
- **Glide** should feel like learning to survive the fall instead of denying it.
- **Air Dodge** should feel like breathing room.
- **Grapple** should feel like being pulled upward by trust, structure, or support.
- **Wall Cling / Wall Jump mastery** should feel like endurance against hard surfaces without immediately falling away.

Movement should tell the same story the plot tells: Aen does not get his old life back. He learns new ways to move through what happened.

## Vertical Combat Implication
Because movement is defense, combat spaces should be designed vertically as well as horizontally. The player should be able to:
- fight while wall-clinging
- attack during grapples
- air-dodge through pressure
- preserve momentum during aerial strikes

If combat ever forces the player into flat-footed repetition for too long, the game is drifting away from its core identity.
