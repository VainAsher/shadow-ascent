# Economy

## Current Truth
The project already has the beginnings of a practical economy surface through inventory, shop, crafting, item unlocks, resource bundles, charms, weapon upgrades, and map or passive reward effects. The economy exists, but it is not the ideological center of the game.

## Design Rule
The economy should support recovery, preparation, and expression. It should not turn Shadow Ascent into a loot treadmill or a grind-first progression game.

## Expected Economic Layers
- currency for buying and selling through merchants
- crafting materials for practical upgrades and special builds
- quest rewards that unlock items, charms, buffs, cosmetics, and map layers
- gear progression that reflects repair, preparation, and support rather than pure wealth accumulation

## Tone Of The Economy
Economy should feel grounded in the world:
- merchants supply continuity and everyday life
- smiths and forge figures tie upgrades to repair and craft
- rare materials should feel narratively placed, not randomly showered
- support-driven rewards should matter because of what they mean, not only because numbers go up

## Balance Principle
The economy should remain secondary to:
- movement progression
- emotional and narrative progression
- authored world discovery

Players should feel helped by the economy, not trapped inside it.

## Current Limitation
The repo proves a shop/crafting/inventory foundation and reward-effect system, but not a final tuned full-campaign economy. This section should be treated as intended structure, not closed balancing truth.
