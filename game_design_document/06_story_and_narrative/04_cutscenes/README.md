# Cutscenes

## Design Position
Cutscenes should be used sparingly and only where staging, timing, or emotional control matter more than uninterrupted play. This game works best when the player embodies Aen's recovery directly, so non-interactive sequences should support major wounds, reveals, and releases rather than replace gameplay storytelling.

## Best Uses
- the Veil Maiden's reveal and the Siren of Masks transition
- the scripted taking of Yin and Yang
- the fall into the Hollow Depths
- key companion return beats where framing matters
- the Hollow Reflection release beat
- the Beacon of Return ending

## What Cutscenes Should Avoid
- explaining lore that rooms, dialogue, or environmental storytelling can already carry
- interrupting movement flow too often in Lantern Heights, Ember Monastery, or Skyroad
- overusing cinematic control in a game whose core strength is embodied traversal and consequence-aware play

## Working Rule
If a scene's emotional force depends on player control, keep it playable. If a scene depends on precise reveal, silence, framing, or irreversible transition, a cutscene is justified.
